PU-ETF

An independent framework exploring how marking decisions could be made more consistent, reviewable and fair.

RoleIndependent author · Policy and administrative design
StatusIndependent proposal · Ongoing drafting
Year2026
Evidence status: Student-authored proposal, not an approved Panjab University scheme or a validated marking intervention.
MARKREVIEWRECORD

The question
behind the work.

When a student disputes an exam mark, can the institution explain the criterion applied, the record of marking and the route for a meaningful review? The framework treats transparent evaluation as an administrative-design problem, not merely a request for more marks.

What the draft proposes

My earlier résumé documents a 276-page version of PU-ETF. Drafting has continued since then, so the earlier page count should not be taken as a definitive current count.

Marking standardsPaper-specific criteria, partial credit, alternative valid reasoning and alignment with learning outcomes.
Examiner calibrationModeration, inter-rater review, drift detection, workload safeguards and reviewer competence.
Student access and appealsAnswer-book access, question-wise re-evaluation, structured grievance grounds and transparent procedures.
Careful digital adoptionTraceable on-screen marking, auditable logs, privacy controls, vendor independence and manual continuity.

Why a pilot matters

The draft proposes beginning with a deliberately limited, subject-diverse pilot rather than treating an individual student's disputed results as representative evidence. It calls for examiner consultation, legal-route mapping, total-cost analysis and published reliability indicators.

Evidence boundary

These are design proposals, not findings from a randomized assessment trial. The source document refers to an individual answer-book and RTI experience as problem context, while acknowledging that broader decisions require independent review and representative evidence.

Institutional reform requires the institution's own scrutiny, authority and evidence—not simply a well-written draft.
