# Evidence Boundaries in Public Examination Reporting

**Exploratory documentary case study · Research working report · November 2026**

## Research question

In selected public reports, when are numerical corrections or process-stage announcements presented as changed administrative outcomes, and what source evidence distinguishes warranted statements from overclaims?

## Method

The study compares official Indian public-examination notices with selected secondary reports, keeping four things separate: **the source**, **the object measured or changed**, **the process stage**, and **what the evidence actually warrants**.

This is a **purposive, AI-assisted, single-workflow documentary analysis**, not a statistically representative media survey. It uses a 12-item diagnostic comparison set and a separate 10-slot retrospective outlet/event lookup grid. The collections overlap and **must not be pooled** to estimate reporting error rates.

## Three contrasting cases

| Case | Official record establishes | Interpretive boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SSC CGL Tier-I 2025, January 2026 corrigendum | List-III table total corrected from 130,418 to 131,027; SSC says no candidates were added to or removed from the previously published list | Corrected *table* does not itself mean changed *candidate membership* |
| NTA NEET-UG 2026, July notices | 10,28,223 candidates updated and confirmed bank details (7 July); 11,46,401 submitted bank details (14 July); refund processing was initiated | Bank details supplied or processing started do not establish that every candidate received a refund |
| NTA CUET-UG 2025, May notice | 76 candidates at one Srinagar centre, on one shift, were affected; an examination would be rescheduled | Prospective scheduling is not evidence that a resit was completed |

Two distinct claim issues are visible in the selected reporting: (1) **direct source contradiction** about a list-change event, and (2) **outcome-stage escalation** where an article asserts completed refunds not demonstrated by its identified notice. Other selected reports preserve the correct distinctions, providing important negative comparisons.

**Important epistemic difference:** A direct contradiction of an issuer's explicit statement is **not the same** as a claim the identified source merely fails to establish. The study does not independently verify candidate rosters, bank settlements or the causes of particular articles' wording.

## Coding approach

- **Direct contradiction:** the identified official notice explicitly rejects the reported proposition.
- **Unsupported completion:** a report states a completed outcome where the identified notice documents only a preceding process stage; this does not prove the outcome false.
- **Aligned:** the report preserves the focal document's object, scope and stage.
- **Uncertain/not located:** source inaccessible, ambiguous or not retrieved; no negative inference from a missing link.

## What this project does *not* claim

- No representative error rate or statistical significance.
- No independent human adjudication or original publication-day archive.
- No evidence of journalists' motives or readers' resulting behaviour.
- No algorithmic superiority over a careful expert source-checklist.
- No peer review, journal acceptance or proof of a novel general provenance theory.
